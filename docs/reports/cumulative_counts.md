# What ISO's Cumulative Counts Do to Detection (prompt 80)

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Prompt:** 80 (`docs/prompts/cc-prompt-80-cumulative-counts.md`)
**Branch:** `phase5/cumulative-counts`
**Status:** measured. No stop condition was met; the four predictions hold (P1). Cumulative
counting would change no conclusion of the Phase 5 gate (section 9).

ISO 14644-1's limits are cumulative: the limit at 0.3 um counts every particle at or above
0.3 um, and a real counter's size channels report those cumulative counts. The monitor and the
arrival maps compare each size class's own concentration with the limit for its size, which
ADR-015 never examined. This pass measures what the cumulative reading does to detection
latency and to the layouts' ranking, on the stored 320x120 frames of prompt 79 and on short
step-resolved reruns of prompt 79's three 80x30 placement runs. Nothing under `src/` changes.

The tables below are generated by `docs/reports/probe80/tables80.py` from the run files and
copied here between markers; `--check` regenerates and compares. The full set, with the
per-unit and per-sensor brackets (B1, B3), the reach and shift per run and channel (D1) and the
searches (L2, K2), is in `docs/reports/probe80/tables80.md`.

## 1. The reading of the classes

The model's five classes are single sizes (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1 and 5 um). This pass takes each to
stand for the particles nearest its size, so a counter's channel at class k's size, which counts
every particle at or above that size, reads the sum of class k and every larger class. That sum
is compared with class k's threshold, the product file's ISO Class 5 value for that size. The
per-class reading, the monitor's and the arrival maps' today, is class k alone against the same
threshold. Under this reading a channel's cumulative count is never below its own class's
concentration, so a cumulative alarm is never later than a per-class one, and the 5 um channel,
with no larger class, is the 5 um class alone.

Both readings come from one function (`probe80.channel`), which sums the classes a channel
names starting from zero, so the per-class reading is the class's own field bit for bit. The
arrival rule is `monitor.ArrivalTracker`'s, unchanged: per event, the first update at or after
its onset at or above threshold (`probe80.ChannelTracker`).

## 4. The brackets on the product grid

The prompt asks for a bracket on each cumulative latency: no earlier than the last frame before
its first frame at or above, and no later than the earlier of that frame and the run's
step-resolved per-class latency "of every class j >= k". **The second half does not hold as
written.** A larger class's step map records when that class reaches its own threshold, which is
lower than channel k's (C0: 29.3 per cubic metre at 5 um against 100,000 at 0.1 um), so a larger
class at its own threshold does not put the sum at channel k's. Only class k's own step arrival
bounds the sum from above: class k at its threshold puts the sum there. The brackets use that
one. A larger class's arrival against channel k's threshold is not stored. Two further pieces are
exact and are used: the 5 um channel is the 5 um class, so its cumulative latency is the march's
at every step; and the file's four have every-step readings in the run files, so their cumulative
latencies are computed exactly from those.

The lower end is a bound only if the sum does not rise to the threshold and fall back between
two frames before its first frame at or above. The 80x30 runs test that (section 5, K4).

What the brackets decide (B2; per unit in B1 and per sensor in B3, tables80.md): for each searched
layout of record, one unit is decided earlier (the dust's 0.5 um channel, whose cumulative frame lies
before the class's own step arrival), the four 5 um units are exact and unchanged, the file's
four are exact and detect nothing, the surface search's door and seal-leak units are never
reached at any frame, and every other unit is left open, its bracket at most a frame wide. The
product layout's worst case is decided to within 3 ms: its cumulative worst case lies in
[2.562, 2.565] s, the lower end being the breach's 5 um unit, which cannot change. That decides
(b). The free optimum's worst case is left open, between 0.145 and 0.399 s: its worst unit is
the dust's 0.1 um channel, which the larger classes can reach before the 0.1 um class does.

## 9. Would cumulative counting change a conclusion of the Phase 5 gate?

No. Each detection conclusion of `docs/reports/phase5_alert_system_report.md` ("The latency
report" and "What Alex may want to decide") holds under the cumulative reading:

- The file's four detect none of the twenty units: holds, exactly, from their every-step readings
  on 320x120 and the step maps on 80x30 (P1 (a)).
- On the surfaces a searched four detect the breach and the dust and neither the door nor the
  seal leak: holds at the frames (L2 in tables80.md), and no surface candidate at any of prompt
  76's standoffs reaches a door unit (D2); on 80x30 at the steps the door is uncoverable (K2). The
  frames cannot exclude an alarm between frames (K4).
- With stands the product search detects all twenty within the breach's worst case, and the seal
  leak within a second: holds; the worst case moves by at most 3 ms (B2) and every latency is at
  or before its per-class value (S1).
- The ranking holds on both grids: holds (O1). The searched layouts move one cell by the mean's
  tie-break, never in the worst case.
- Every detecting layout declares the room clean early: holds, with the intervals equal or longer
  (A1, A2).

What does change is the size of some latencies, the dust's above all: the cumulative 0.1 um
channel sees the dust release where the 0.1 um class alone barely does, and the free optimum's
worst case on 80x30 falls by about two fifths (K1). Phase 5 claims the ranking, not absolute
latencies, so this does not touch a claim.

**What a build would touch.** Not recommended here; listed for the decision.

- `src/monitor.py`: `AlertMonitor.update` would read each channel's sum against its threshold,
  so the alert table, the readings it reports and `latencies` change meaning;
  `ArrivalTracker.update` would track the sums. The sensor series in the run file could stay per
  class, with the sums derived.
- The arrival maps: `placement.arrival_from_frames` and `clear_times` would sum the classes the
  same way, and `arrival_from_run`'s fallback with them.
- The run file's maps: `/arrival/` and `/transport/alerts/` would hold the cumulative reading,
  which needs a marker (an attribute naming the counting) so that the files made before it are
  read as per-class and are not mixed.
- REQ-A03 and ADR-015 C ("Thresholds per class"): the thresholds stay per size, but the
  comparison is with the channel's sum, and the reading of the classes in section 1 above (each
  class stands for the particles nearest its size) becomes a stated assumption of the model.
- What it would have to re-run: every run whose `/arrival/` or alert table a table reads, that is
  prompt 79's five 320x120 runs and three 80x30 placement runs, and the sweep's arm runs if K2's
  arm reading is kept; then tables76/tables79's re-scores. The stored frames hold every class, so
  the frame-resolved readings (K2, the all-clear tables) could be rebuilt without new runs, and
  only the step-resolved maps and alert tables need the marches again.

## 10. Deviations and notes

- **The bracket's upper end** uses class k's own step arrival only; the prompt's "every class
  j >= k" does not bound the sum (section 4).
- **The clean-out** is in the maps and the structural check but not in the scored units, as in
  prompt 76: every cell is at or above every threshold at its onset, t = 0.
- **The frames, a class and a frame at a time** on 320x120. The 80x30 calibration and the
  comparisons with `arrival_from_frames` and `clear_times` load the small runs' frames whole.
- **Section 8** (the all-clear reading) and D2 (the door at other standoffs) are beyond the
  prompt's sections; they were added so that section 9 could answer for every gate conclusion
  from measurements.
- **Run cost.** The three 80x30 probe runs took 43 to 90 s of wall time each, all three at
  once; `tables80.py` regenerates everything in about a minute.
