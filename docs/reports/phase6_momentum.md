## 1. The port

`core/include/cfdcore/momentum.hpp` and `core/src/momentum.cpp` hold
`momentum::predictor<D>`, instantiated at 1 and 2 (section 7) and described here for the two
directions the reference has. It is constructed once on a grid with the mesh's widths, air's density and
viscosity, the relaxation, the sweep count, each component's two transverse edge conditions and
the vent faces. The binding exposes it as `_cfdcore.MomentumPredictor2`;
`tests/core_mapping.py` builds one from the reference's own arguments. `predict` returns u*, v*
and the two diagonals as new arrays.

**In place, by rows.** The reference writes one component's arithmetic with its own axis last and
transposes every array for v. The core keeps the reference's names for a component's own axis, s,
and the transverse one, t, and walks every field by its rows, the lines along x, for both
components (prompt 87's layout). Three layouts hold everything: the cells, where the streamwise
faces of the momentum control volumes lie; the corners [ny + 1, nx + 1], where the transverse
faces lie for both components and which is `wall_mu`'s layout; and the component's own faces. A
quantity that varies along s is read per element in a u row and as the row's one value in a v row,
and the other way about for t, chosen at compile time by the component.

**What is fixed, and what a call does.**

| When | What | The reference's counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| construction | the QUICK weights of both directions with their boundary forms at the domain's ends, beyond an obstacle's wall node and beside an obstacle face | `quick_face_values`' weights, formed every call; `_obstacle_streamwise`'s and `_obstacle_transverse`'s, formed every call |
| construction | the obstacle walls: the faces of the wall stencil, their distances, the unknowns whose neighbour is an obstacle's, the cells and corners where QUICK takes the boundary form, the faces whose wall viscosity an unknown reads | `_obstacle_walls` and `_wall_faces`, held from construction; the blocked masks, formed every call |
| construction | the unknown faces, the edges' conditions, the vent faces as masks; the distance each transverse face's conductance divides by; the viscosity means' fractions; the scalar path's conductances | formed every call from `_Orientation`'s arrays, except the conditions |
| every call | the checks; then per component the rows of fluxes, the rows of equations and the sweeps | `predict` |

A component's equations are formed in one walk of its rows of unknowns. The fluxes through the
row of streamwise faces and the row of transverse faces are formed as the walk reaches them (the
mass flux, the conductance, the upwind coefficients, the QUICK minus upwind flux, and with a
viscosity field the corner viscosity and the stress flux), and the row of unknowns reads them: from
the row of cells and the corner rows below and above it for u, from the cell rows below and above
and the one corner row for v. Two rows of each are kept and take turns. The sweeps then run on the
assembled coefficients, the source with its relaxation term held at the call's input, as `_sweep_n`
runs them; one sweep is the same code, and `_sweep` and `_sweep_n` agree for one.

**Where the order decides bits.** Every value is elementwise, so the port is the reference's
expressions in the reference's order: the diagonal's neighbour sum s+, s-, t+, t- and its net flux
in the same order; QUICK's three products with the far node's first; the deferred correction
negated before the stress is added; the source as `((b_boundary + b_deferred) + b_pressure) +
((1 - alpha) / alpha a_P) phi`; the quotient-times-width of the transverse stress flux, which
rounds twice and does not cancel. A face is an unknown of the sweeps where its diagonal is
positive, as the reference tests it, and not where the mesh says so: a control volume every mass
flux enters has a diagonal the fluxes' rounding can take to zero or below, and the reference then
writes zero there (the planted "wide" flows reach it). `np.maximum(x, 0.0)` is `positive_part`: a
NaN stays and -0.0 becomes 0.0.

**The refusals.** The binding raises the reference's own, with its types and messages, in its
order: the fields' shapes, then `mu_eff`'s type, dtype, shape, finiteness and sign, then
`wall_mu`'s type, keys and, for u and then v, the same of each field at the faces an unknown
reads. The value checks are the core's (`check_viscosity`, `check_wall_viscosity`), which return a
status. The binding's own are the ones the reference has no need of: an array that is not a
C-contiguous, aligned float64 one, and a `transient` argument, a `NotImplementedError`, since the
transient prediction stays on the reference (ADR-017 F). `stencil_viscosity` is the corner
viscosity's own code, kept at the faces an unknown reads.

## 3. The kernels

**Where the port follows CLAUDE.md's rules.** Every pass is a block function on a row: the operands
in a struct the row's walker takes by value (or a few pointers, for the short ones), the outputs
`CFDCORE_RESTRICT` parameters, the choices `select` and `keep` on masks of 64 bits, a row's end
cells the same block on one cell with operands that hold the boundary (the missing far node, the
zero beyond the row, the edge's value). Neighbours along the row are read either side of one
pointer between a row's ends. The unknowns beside a domain edge, whose known neighbour enters the
source or whose coefficient leaves the diagonal, are a block of their own after the diagonal's (in
6ca02d7 they were instantiations of one block of every coefficient). No pass copies a field per sweep: the sweeps write the output and one
scratch iterate in turn, the last always the output. Every array is allocated at construction.

**Where it departs, and why.**

- The rows of fluxes are kept in buffers between passes, about a dozen passes to a row of unknowns
  where one could be written. A single block would read many more streams than the processor has
  registers for; the buffers are a row long.
- The reference's `np.maximum(x, 0.0)` is `positive_part` and not `scalar::np_maximum(x, 0.0)`,
  which GCC 13 folds against the constant zero into a branch, so that the two flux blocks stay
  scalar (T6, `np_maximum`).
- The two components take turns in one set of arrays. Arrays of their own were measured beside it
  (T6, `two_scratches`).
- The coefficients of a row are written in a pass a direction and the diagonal in one more, where
  6ca02d7 wrote them all in one block (T8, `coefficients`, whose time includes every one of these
  passes): a block's outputs are parameters, so a block that wrote every direction's would have a
  parameter list that changes with D.

The QUICK face values of both directions are mostly choices, so both forms were timed under the
project's flags, as the rules ask. There was no build of the commit before 6ca02d7 that had these kernels, so
the comparison T6 makes is between forms built from 6ca02d7's source with the edits
`variants92.FORMS` listed, timed in alternating order in one session on the same captured call. T8
(section 7) is the restructure's comparison with 6ca02d7.

## 7. Round 1: the module per direction

**Alex's decision (2026-11-23).** The momentum module takes the number of directions as a
parameter, as the core's other modules do (ADR-017 decision 4), so that it is ready to generalise
to the true 3D model. In this report's words of prompt 92 the module was built for two directions
only "because the stencil has one transverse direction"; in 3D each component has two.

**The form.** `predictor<D>` is a template on D like `grid`, `layout` and `scalar`, instantiated at
2 for the binding and at 1 for its own tests (a straight duct: one component, no transverse
direction, its equations written out by hand in `core/tests/test_momentum.cpp`). Component c moves
along direction c. What needs one direction (the streamwise fluxes, QUICK's streamwise values, the
pressure) is held and formed per component. What needs two (the corner's viscosity, form b's
transverse stress, the obstacle's transverse rule, the wall stencil across a transverse face, the
domain edges' conditions, `wall_mu`) is held in a `pair_setup` and formed by functions of `<C, T>`,
one for each ordered pair of a component and a direction transverse to it. The assembly walks a
component's rows by their positions and, at each row, the component's pairs; a block that sums over
directions (the diagonal, the source, a sweep) folds over the transverse ones at compile time, so
that for two directions it is the expression it was, and a row of fluxes is kept for as long as the
walk needs the last along the direction it crosses.

A direction lies along the rows or crosses them; a block is written for both and knows nothing
else of which direction it is given. `for_each_row` walks positions, not (j, i).

**The values.** Every one is as it was, bit for bit: CTest, the parity module, the 40 captured
`predict` and 30 `stencil_viscosity` calls (T3, and the tester's own replay of them, which maps the
arguments by a route of its own), and the tester's two solves driven by the core's `predict` in the
reference's place, the 40x40 cavity (3,435 outer iterations) and the 80x30 product room (1,021),
whose stops, residual histories and fields are the reference's
(`results/builder92b/tester_final/`).

**The speed.** `probe92.py kernels` builds 6ca02d7 and the checkout side by side and times each
block and each pass alone on 320x120 rows (`kernels92b.cpp`), the two builds in alternating order,
eight placements of the arrays on the heap, three sessions.

## Findings

- **F1, the viscosity field needs no masked copy.** `_check_mu_eff` returns a copy with SOLID cells
  at air's viscosity so that the harmonic mean stays defined on faces that are not unknowns. The
  core reads the caller's field as it is: a mean of two SOLID cells may be anything, and the masks
  that make a face not an unknown keep it out of every output. A plant showed it, and the planted
  cases hold a NaN in every SOLID cell.
- **F2, a diagonal that is not positive at an open face is a case the reference has.** Where every
  mass flux enters a control volume, the upwind coefficients and the net flux cancel to the
  conductances, and fluxes large enough lose those to rounding: the diagonal is then zero or
  negative, the reference takes the face for one that is not an unknown, and writes zero. No
  captured call comes near it. The core tests the diagonal's sign, not the mesh's mask, so it does
  the same.
- **F3, GCC 13 folds a maximum against a constant zero into a branch.** `scalar::np_maximum(x, 0.0)`
  left both flux blocks scalar; the comparison written so that its mask is kept did not. The
  vectorisation report showed it and the parity tests could not, which is what CLAUDE.md's rule
  about reading the report is for.
- **F4, the reference's per-call values can be read without wrapping each function.** A profile hook
  that keeps a function's local values as it returns gives the set-up comparison every intermediate
  the reference forms inside `predict`, the weights and the fractions among them, so prediction (b)
  is scored on what the call computed and not on a second evaluation in the test.

## Deviations and limits

- **The timings are of this machine in the state T5 and T6 record**, the processor below its nominal
  frequency.
- **Prompt 92's `predict` had no build of the commit before to be timed against**; T6 compares forms
  of it, and T8 round 1's with prompt 92's. The kernels of the steps before are in other files, which this pass does not touch
  (`core/python/bindings.cpp` gains a class and changes none).
- **Compiled with GCC 13.3 only**, with warnings as errors (`scripts/build_core.sh`). CI's core job
  uses the runner's GCC; its result on the pull request is the check of that compiler, and nothing
  there says whether a block was left scalar.
- **NaN inputs are compared by position and by the bits of every other value**; a NaN's payload and
  sign are the processor's. The reference does not refuse a NaN in u, v or p, and neither does the
  core.
- **The predictor does not bring the flow state into the run.** Its arguments and outputs cross the
  binding at each call; the resident flow state is step 5's.
- **The constructor refuses more than the reference's.** It refuses a width, a wall distance, rho or mu that
  is not finite and positive and an alpha outside (0, 1] (round 1, review 92 S3); the reference does
  not, since `SimConfig` has refused them by then. Every public call of the core refuses an array of
  another length (S4).
- **`stencil_viscosity` forms the corner viscosities that `predict` forms again.** The reference does
  the same. `flow` (step 5), which calls both with one field, could form them once.
- **The module is instantiated at D = 1 and 2 only** (section 7). At three directions it has been
  run in a scratch program on extruded 2D rooms and on nothing else, and a transverse face's area
  there lacks the third width.
- **The plants of prompt 92 were run again on round 1's code, and three are other edits for the same
  defect** where the line they edited is gone: `diagonal_order` and `flux_order` group a transverse
  direction's two terms in place of moving them to the front, since the order is now the fold's;
  `sweep_order` likewise.
- **The captured calls are ten outer iterations a room.** The product and vents rooms start from
  converged states, so their calls differ in the last digits only (T2's last column); the Annex 20
  room's field moves by tens of centimetres per second over its ten. The planted cases carry the
  rest.
- **The cavity runs the laminar path with one sweep, as its configuration has it**; the other three run
  ten.
- **Nothing under `src/` or `configs/` changed.** The stop conditions were not met: no expression
  needed a change there, and no step needed `sudo`.
