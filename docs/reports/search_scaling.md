# How Far the Exact Placement Search Scales (prompt 82)

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Prompt:** 82 (`docs/prompts/cc-prompt-82-search-scaling.md`)
**Branch:** `phase5/search-scaling`
**Status:** measured. Predictions (b) and (c) hold, (a) and (d) fail (the findings below). The
stop condition is not met: the enumeration and the built set cover agree on the worst case
everywhere both ran.

The Phase 5 follow-up scores the dust release from several positions on the tool tops. Each
position adds five units (one per channel) to the four scenarios' twenty. The worst-case rule's
exact search (`placement._worst_case_cover`) keeps one entry per subset of the units, so its
cost doubles per unit, and it refuses above `MAX_SET_COVER_UNITS = 20`. This pass measures how
far that search, an exact enumeration over the maximal coverage masks, and the mean rule's
greedy search carry on this laptop. Nothing under `src/` changes; the probe is
`docs/reports/probe82/`, and no scenario is run.

The tables below are generated by `docs/reports/probe82/tables82.py` from the records in
`results/builder82/` and copied here between markers; `--check` regenerates and compares. The
full set is `docs/reports/probe82/tables82.md`.

**What the tables are.** The latency tables (candidates by units) are built with main's code
on cumulative maps, over the product search's candidates (the product file's mount, standoff
and stand band). The 320x120 table is the four event scenarios of prompt 81. The 160x60 tables
are the sweep's finished arm runs of prompt 79, read from their stored frames on the channels,
in a fixed order: by arm (control, i10, max_nut, min_nut, sc02, widest), then by scenario
(door, breach, dust), each run's channels in order, a table of N units being the first N. **The
160x60 runs stand in for further release positions and are not other release positions: they
are other turbulence arms of the same three scenarios.** What is measured is the search's cost
and its answer's quality on tables of realistic structure, not any layout.

## The findings

1. **(a) fails: memory stops the built set cover before time does.** Its peak is the subset
   table and the arrays over it, 41 to 42 bytes a subset (C1, against the 45 the estimate
   allows), so it doubles per unit, and so does its time (C2). It finishes at 27 units at every
   count within the limits; at 28 the estimate is above 8 GB and the case was not run, where
   the line through the measured sizes (C3) puts the time well inside 30 minutes. The same
   line puts 30 units, three positions, above both limits.
2. **On the realistic tables the problem is easy, and so the enumeration's cost on them is not
   a test of its hard case.** At two to four sensors the optimum equals the floor (the latest
   unit's earliest detection by any candidate) on every realistic table, and there are two
   maximal masks at the optimum and at the largest latency on every one of them (M1). M2 shows
   the structure: within each run the channels the candidates detect are nested (every
   candidate's set of the run's channels is a subset or a superset of every other's; which
   channel comes first differs from run to run), and the two maximal masks are disjoint, so
   they split the runs into two groups that no candidate spans. The stand-ins repeat
   the same three release positions, so each added run joins one of the two groups and adds no
   new hard unit. Distinct release positions on the tool tops may not behave so, and the tables
   here cannot say.
3. **The noise bound** (independent latencies, the most diverse a table can be; added by the
   builder, not a realistic table) is where the enumeration does work. Its maximal masks
   approach the candidates (M1), and its time at four sensors rises from under a second at 20
   units to past 30 minutes at 40 and 60, where it was stopped (C1). It is still faster than
   the built cover wherever both ran (20 and 24 units), and holds a few MB where the built
   cover holds the subset table. At three sensors it finishes at every size.
4. **The enumeration and the built set cover agree**: the same worst case and the same cells in
   every case where both ran, realistic and noise, and the same worst case and cells as the
   exhaustive search at one and two sensors wherever a cover exists (A1).
5. **(d) fails at two sensors only.** On the realistic tables the greedy's worst case equals
   the exact optimum at three and four sensors, and at two it is above it by more than 10% on
   nine of the ten tables (G1); `search` itself never runs the greedy at two (it takes every
   pair). On the noise bound the greedy is above the optimum by more than 10% in nine of the
   ten cases at three and four sensors.
6. **How many dust release positions each method carried at four sensors** (N1, five units a
   position on top of the other scenarios' fifteen): the built set cover two on the realistic
   tables (the noise bound has no table between 24 and 28 units); the enumeration nine on the
   realistic tables (the largest measured; the masks as the built code forms them, int64, hold
   63 units, nine positions, without a change) and three on the noise bound; the greedy nine
   on both, its worst case the optimum's at three and four sensors on the realistic tables and
   not on the noise bound (G1). The enumeration's nine rests on tables whose structure M2
   shows to be easy; what distinct release positions would give lies between the two series
   and was not measured.

**The enumeration** (`probe82.enumerate_cover`). The bisection over the sorted distinct
latencies, the masks, the maximal masks, the cover enumeration at the optimum
(`placement._covers`) and the tie-break by the mean with its exchanges are the built
`_worst_case_cover`'s, called or copied line for line. One thing is replaced: the subset table
`_fewest`, 2^units entries, by `Feasibility`, which decides whether at most k maximal masks
cover a set of units. It tries every combination implicitly: every cover of a target holds a
mask containing each of its units, so it branches on the masks that contain the target's unit
fewest masks contain, cuts the masks to the target and keeps the maximal cuts at each step
(which changes no answer), tests every mask at once with one slot left and every pair at once
with two, and keeps each answer by (target, slots). `_covers` reads the subset table only to
compare it with the slots left, so `Fewest` answers it from `Feasibility` on the subsets it
asks about. `test_probe82.py` checks `Feasibility` against brute force over every combination
(40 random sets of masks, slots 0 to 4), `Fewest` against `_fewest` on every subset (10), and
the enumeration's cells against the built cover's at two to four sensors and against the
argmin's worst case at one (60 random tables, plumes and noise): 111 checks, all pass.

**What adopting the enumeration in `src/` would touch.** No recommendation is made here.

- `src/placement.py`: `_worst_case_cover` (the feasibility test in its bisection, and the
  refusal above `MAX_SET_COVER_UNITS` with its message); `_fewest` and `_UNION_CHUNK`, used
  only there; `_covers`, whose `fewest` argument is an array indexed by subset; the constant
  `MAX_SET_COVER_UNITS` and its comment (the limit would become the masks' width: the int64
  masks `bits` forms hold 63 units, and more needs a wider mask); `search`'s docstring (Notes,
  the dynamic programme; Raises, the cap). `Layout.distinct_masks` and `maximal_masks` keep
  their meaning. The only caller in `src/` is `search_roles`, through `search`.
- `tests/test_placement.py`: `test_more_units_than_the_set_cover_holds_are_refused` pins the
  20-unit refusal; the mask counts the tests assert (`distinct_masks`, `maximal_masks`) are
  counted the same way and should not move; the feasibility test would need its own tests (the
  probe's are the shape).
- `docs/SYSTEM.md`, `search`'s contract: "more than MAX_SET_COVER_UNITS (20) units in the set
  cover" among its ValueErrors.
- ADR-015 D: the search bullet (dynamic programming over the subsets), the as-built paragraph
  (the refusal above 20 units), fix pass (6) on memory, and note (3) of the round after prompt
  76 (the exact search stops at 20 units; several release positions need the greedy or a larger
  cap measured first).
- The worst-case optimum would no longer be bounded by the units at all, but by the
  enumeration's own cost, which grows with the maximal masks and the count (the noise bound,
  C1); on a table where the masks multiply its time is not bounded by anything measured here.

## 7. Deviations and notes

1. **At one sensor neither the built cover nor the greedy runs.** Both end in
   `_exchanged`, which holds the other sensors fixed, and at one sensor there are none, so it
   raises; `search` never calls them there (it takes the argmin). Those cases are not in the
   queue (the greedy's were in the first measured queue, raised, and their records were
   removed with them; `queue.jsonl` still lists them); the enumeration at one sensor skips the exchange (`enumerate_cover`), and its worst
   case is checked against the argmin's. At one sensor no candidate detects every unit on any
   table, so the worst case is inf there.
2. **The greedy at two sensors** is measured, though `search` takes every pair at two; (d) is
   judged as stated, over every count where the exact optimum is known, and G1 marks the counts.
3. **One BLAS thread in the case processes.** OpenBLAS commits a buffer per thread at import,
   about 0.8 GB of private bytes on this machine, which would sit under every peak; no method
   here calls BLAS, and `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` keeps the baseline near 32 MB.
4. **The 30-minute limit and the 8 GB limit** are the prompt's; the machine has 63 GB, so the
   memory limit, not the machine, stopped the built cover at 28 units.
5. **The first queue's records were discarded.** Its memory counter called
   `K32GetProcessMemoryInfo` without declared argument types and every case failed before
   measuring; nothing it wrote was kept. The cases in the tables are from the two queues after
   the fix (`results/builder82/queue.out`, `queue2.out`), the second for the edge and noise
   tables added afterwards.
